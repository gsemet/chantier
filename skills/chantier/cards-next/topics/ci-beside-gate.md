# CI beside the long gate

- The project's rules name a pipeline job as the long gate: run no `gate`. Push and open the PR after the approved round (the first push starts the job; `ship`'s `off: no long gate passed at <sha>` is expected), read CI (`help ci`), and once that job is green at the PR's head with the `VERIFIED` verdict there, `handback <cr> ready <link> … --no-gate "<the rule>"`, quoting the rule. A red run of that job is a red CI job: a round. A rebase needs no gate carry: the job runs again at the pushed head. GitLab and Copilot: unproved.
- The `ci: beside the long gate` line (a CR Driver only): after the approved round, the commit and the rebase, push, open or update the PR as a draft (the audit pass on `pr-body.md` first), `ship`, then start the long gate (`$CHANTIER gate <cr> <name>…`) and the CI watch (`gh pr checks <pr> --watch --fail-fast`; GitLab `glab ci status --live`, unproved), each in its own background shell, do what needs neither, and end your turn: the first to end wakes you.
  - A red job that is the CR's: at once `gate <cr> --stop` (recorded `stopped`, not a round), a `note` naming the job and the head, the log to `<work folder>/ci-<run>.log`, a `debugger`, the next round.
  - An infrastructure failure, or a job also red on the default branch: `help trouble`; the gate is not stopped.
  - The gate fails first: a round, and stop the CI watch's shell by its id. The gate passes first: wait for CI, and the reverse. Both done, the gate passed and CI green or judged by the rules: the `verifier` at the pushed head.
  - The early push of an existing PR (`help conflicts-early-push`) uses the same outcomes.
- With that line, read from the forge's checks the systems CI covers (a job name such as `just check (ubuntu-latest)` names its system) and compare them with the system the long gate ran on. Known gaps gets one bullet naming the systems only CI covers, for example "The long gate ran on Windows only; Linux and macOS are covered by CI alone." A forge that lists no check gives no such bullet.
