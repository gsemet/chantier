# Chaining a queued CR

You may chain a queued CR on another when that costs less than rebasing: small work, or two CRs whose specifications edit the same lines (one table, one template block), whose rebase would conflict anyway. A shared file alone is no reason, and a CR that has started is never chained.
Send `[foreman→chief-of-staff] chain <cr> after <cr>: <reason>` and leave the CR queued. The Chief of Staff records it with `order after` and `order note`, and the user may turn it off while the CR is queued. Once the CR has started, the chain stands until the stack driver restacks (in a series or a parallel batch, until its rebase note). A started CR whose dependency went back to a Driver is restacked by the stack driver (series or parallel: it gets the rebase note).
- A chain is an `after` with no real dependency, yours or one the user's go approved; `order after <cr> --note -` ends it while the CR is queued. `take` refuses a queued CR until each CR of its `after` (in a series its predecessor too) is ready or merged; a cancelled one stays unmet: ask the user, who drops the CR or changes it. A release's `after` is met by ready or merged in a stack or series batch, by merged in a parallel one; a cancelled CR never holds it back. `stack` refuses an order putting a CR below one of its `after`.
