# A worker that fails

- A worker refused for its first line, or ended by a host failure, gets one new start of the same role with the same brief (`brief <role> <cr>` run again, its output passed unchanged, then the same `Note:`): `$CHANTIER note <cr> "restart: <cause>"` first, no `round`. A second refusal of the same start, a model included, is `handback <cr> blocked refused <exact text>`, never retried without the model.
- A reply out of format: never read a verdict, a pass or a choice into it, never fix it, never ask the worker by message to fix it. `note <cr> "restart: reply out of format: <what is missing>"` and one new worker of the same role with the same brief, saying what the reply lacked; no `round`. A second out-of-format reply: a Coder's is the next round; another role's is `handback <cr> blocked question: <role> replied out of format twice: <what is missing>` (the stack driver: `msg steering anomaly`). A worker is never resumed by a message; a new round is always a new child.
- A coder that failed and left a dirty worktree: a new `coder` in the same round, no `round`, its brief naming what the failed attempt left and what to do with it (keep it, fix it, or discard it back to the round's starting head, by the coder's own git). At most one restart per round: a second failed coder is the next round, its brief carrying both failures. A worktree left dirty by a failed rebase goes to a `conflict-resolver`.
- A long reply in format is read, not restarted: `note <cr> "long reply: <role>: <n> lines"` (`<n> characters` for JSON), and that role's next brief says the last reply passed its cap. An extra key alone in an Inspector's JSON is noted the same way.
- Escalation, only when a trigger arises ([concepts](../concepts.md), "Escalation"): read the role's `escalation=` on the `team:` line, then the `escalate:` line of `$CHANTIER playbook --batch <batch>`, as printed. A `debugger` that explains no red check, or a `conflict-resolver` that settles no conflict, gets a second one on the escalation model; a note `escalate: rounds` or `escalate: blocked twice: …` puts each new round's `coder` on its escalation model. You never escalate yourself.
